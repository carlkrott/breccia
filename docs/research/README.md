# Paired research (standing rotation)

Every implementation / test round is paired with **one research subagent** that does two things:

1. **Vulkan capability audit** — anything in the driver/extension/parameter surface we are not exploiting, tied to a current bottleneck.
2. **One engine audit** — a single inference engine (not yet covered) reviewed for workflows, methods and specific advantages we have not tried, with an adaptation sketch for our Vulkan engine.

Findings land in `docs/research/NN-<engine>.md`. This index tracks coverage so we never re-audit the same engine twice.

* **24 — MoE offload with mixed-precision experts: a 2-bit shadow of cold experts cuts expert bytes per token by 25.2% with S1 (IQ2_XXS gate/up, Q2_0 down) and 20.8% with S2 (all Q2_0), derived from the class sizes in the expert index; on the CPU lane, a per-type model built on single-thread kernel rates (not a timing run) cuts lane time by 16.8% for S1 and 2.1% for S2 (UNVERIFIED: the Q2_0 gate/up rate was never measured), so S1 is the better shadow and S2 is rejected as a standalone shadow. Keeping the top-1 expert exact costs 2.5 points of bytes. Accuracy of every 2-bit variant is UNVERIFIED and the round-12 parity gate applies (per-stage rel L2 ≤ 2e-3, top-1 agreement, logit delta ≤ 0.02, PPL +0.3%). Ranked: (1) a per-token route trace with weights and offline replay of static, LFU with hysteresis, decayed frequency and LRU against the round-23 gate (hit ≥ 0.198 and ≥ 5 points over static); (2) tail-skip parity plus a per-type lane timing run at the production thread count; (3) down-only Q2_0 on the 18 IQ4_NL down layers (+1.9–2.6% tok/s, derived). * **23 — Model-agnostic drafting, expert prediction and placement: no drafter or prefetch pays without a measurement. Break-even acceptance at k=3 is 0.42 (doc 21 model), 0.43 with a batched head and 0.56 with a per-row head (derived), so the first gate is a CPU-only acceptance replay of prompt-lookup, suffix and n-gram proposers over recorded text. A streamed expert that hits VRAM saves 72 µs per token (derived): +5% tok/s needs a hit rate of 0.198, and an exposed copy needs 92–97% precision, so prefetch must overlap. Full-depth top-2 self-draft costs d = 0.72 (p* ≥ 0.95, rejected); a quantised draft at the measured int8 ratio never breaks even (d = 0.79). Replicating experts across cards does not shorten the T=1 serial sum; VRAM headroom bounds it at about +9% tok/s (loose derived ceiling; stranded remainder at most about +2.9%), and gen3x4 offload costs 602 µs per expert. Ranked: (1) the CPU acceptance replay, (2) a per-token expert-id trace (`BRECCIA_TRACE_ROUTE` prints distinct counts only), (3) a residual-VRAM measurement before any block move. Speed figures are derived or `UNVERIFIED - requires the GPU qualification card`. Detail: `23-model-agnostic-drafting-and-expert-prediction.md`.**
* **22 — Vulkan driver surface for tiering: the blocker is sync, not queues. Per-block host import (18 imports) beats per-expert (9,216 allocations, derived). A fixed slot table with an expert index beats a general LRU suballocator. Radeon 610M is about 0 (DisplayOnly; it shares DDR5 with a host lane of about 5.9 GB/s, derived). Cooperative matrix stays negative. Every speed claim waits on the qualification card.**

* **12 — quantised GEMV is instruction-issue bound, and the constant is measurable: rate (M el/ms) = 62 400 / `I_unit` on the MI50, 127 000 on the RX.** Static SPIR-V counts of the real decode units (our own `glslc -O` flags, no GPU touched — the paired agent owned both cards) give `IQ3_S` 177 instructions per 8-element unit/lane, `Q5_K`-wide 202, `IQ2_*` 127, `IQ4_XS` 133, `BF16` 49, against a dot that is only **12** of them — the dequant is 92–94 % of the arithmetic, so "vectorise the dot" (half2/`v_pk_*`) is worth ≤4 % and is the wrong target. Two unrelated types that have no stall land on the *same* instruction-slot constant to **2.3 %** (IQ3_S 7.77e12, Q5_K-wide 7.95e12 slots/s ≈ 1.2 slots/cycle/CU), and BF16 lands at 98 % of its DRAM ceiling, so both halves of the model are validated against numbers §8ax already measured. The residual gap is a **dependent-codebook-lookup stall**, monotone in lookups per unit (2 lookups → 100 % of the model, 3 → 69 %, 8 → 47 % for `IQ4_XS`'s per-element float LUT), and `Q5_K` pre-fix sat at 39 % because of sector amplification, not instructions — the same statement read the other way, and the reason the `_wide` build gained 2.25× while *adding* 17 % instructions. **The law**: ALU-bound iff `I_unit > 8·bpw` (MI50) / `53·bpw` (RX) — so every codebook type is ALU bound on the MI50 by 4–5× (bytes almost irrelevant) while the RX runs out of DRAM first, which is the quantitative form of "matched to strengths" (**instruction-bound → RX, 2.0× the slots; bandwidth-bound → MI50, 1.85× the bytes**) and is *already visible* in §8av's `attn_qkv` 2 205 (RX) vs 4 143 (MI50). **Ranked fixes**: (1) int8 dot + `q8_1` activation (**1.9–3.3× on the MI50, ~0–10 % on the RX** — llama.cpp's own arch detection separates RDNA3 from RDNA2 by `integerDotProduct4x8BitPackedMixedSignednessAccelerated`, so gfx1030 gets instruction-count only; the paired probe owns the number, this doc owns the decision rule), whose *algebraic* wins matter more than the MAC: the per-block scale hoists out of the element loop and the `−dmin·m` term collapses to one multiply against the q8_1 block **sum** (`Σx` already in the header) — things no fp32 path can do; (2) the `q8_1` form must come from the *producer* (our `ops_rmsnorm`/`ops_swiglu` already reduce a whole row) or it costs +0.1–0.4 ms/token of launches; (3) packed-nibble unpack keeps 1.2–1.6× **without any extension** (fp32 floor is `I_unit` 70–90, our `Q5_K` is 190); (4) codebook → LDS is a rider for `IQ4_XS` only (`IQ3_S` is at 100 % of its model, so it is a placebo there); (5) **pre-dequantised weight caching is rejected on two independent grounds** — an f16 copy is DRAM bound at 405–413 M el/ms, i.e. *slower than the int8 path* (624–1 248) and only 1.2–1.9× today's quant decode, and the dense set would need **17.0 GiB against 16 GiB per card** (`mem_info_vram_total` 17 163 091 968 B) that already holds 3.66 GiB dense + ~8 GiB of arena. **Accuracy contract**: keep the bit-identical f32 path as default and reference (flag, default off); gate the int8 path on per-stage relative L2 error ≤2e-3 (`max|Δ|/max|y|` ≤5e-3), 8/8 exclusive processes agreeing on the first 8 tokens and ≥7/8 on 32, `max|Δlogit|` ≤0.02 over the top-20 and top-1 probability delta ≤0.005, and PPL within +0.3 % — strictly stronger than llama.cpp, which ships `q8_1` activations with no gate at all on AMD for `k ≥ 2048`. **Top-3**: X1 int8 on IQ3/IQ2 + fused producer **−3.5…−4.5 ms/token**, X2 Q5_K/Q4_K min-collapse + head placement **−1.1…−1.9**, X3 `gated`→`up` fusion + router merge + `IQ4_XS` LDS **−2.2…−2.7** → **47–49 ms, 20.4–21.3 tok/s**.
* **11 — recording is 66 ns/dispatch, so the 6.9 µs is a counter artefact, not a code path; and MLC's Vulkan runtime is TVM's, whose record path is *worse* than ours.** Measured on the headless RX with a purpose-built harness (record loop only, no submits, 12 call combinations, DPM `high` in the header, MI50 never touched): **`vkCmdDispatch` 13.6 ns; +`bind_pipeline` 16.0; +`bind_descriptor_sets` 26.6; +`push_constants`(32 B) 37.3; +sync2 barrier 65.7** — that is our exact 7-call shape, and it is **flat at N = 100/1000/2000**, i.e. per call, not per command buffer; v1 `ALL_COMMANDS` records **+27 ns** on top (92.9) for zero GPU-timeline gain; the same shape via **raw `PFN_` pointers is 0.8 ns *slower***, which **refutes the ash-trampoline theory outright**. Replicating `record()`'s host work call-for-call adds **+202 ns → 266 ns total**, of which **165 ns is the five `Instant::now()` pairs of the profiler itself**, 60–78 ns is `track_disabled()`'s two `env::var_os`, 18 ns the `(u64, Vec<Buffer>)` hash key — and none of it degrades at 4/8/16 threads (2.5 ns/op). **Therefore 6.9 µs cannot be recording: 266 ns × 1 843 = 0.49 ms/token.** The printed figure is `RECORD_NS / runs_n` (`main.rs:2718`) with the numerator reset after the prompt (`graph.rs:3280`) and `sess.dispatches` **never reset** — two windows, and the budget's parts sum to 67.2 ms against 56.9 ms of wall because recording overlaps `wait-gpu` by construction. **Ranked real causes: (d) our own in-path bookkeeping 202 ns > (c) the barrier 29 ns > (b) the descriptor bind 11 ns > (a)/(e)/(f) ≈ 0** — 51 CB boundaries are 204 ns each (10 µs/token), and RADV's barrier2 is cheap to record. **Do not port ncnn's push descriptor**: with a legal `PUSH_DESCRIPTOR` layout `vkCmdPushDescriptorSet` is **+85 ns over `bind_descriptor_sets`**; `VK_EXT_descriptor_buffer` offsets are +5.4 ns and **bindless is −10.9 ns (−20 µs/token)**, so round 10's "D0 is infrastructure, not a win" is confirmed and priced. ggml is the model that matches the floor: a **descriptor-set ring indexed by the dispatch ordinal** with a struct compare and **no `vkUpdateDescriptorSets` in steady state** — adopt that (R3) instead of a hash map. **MLC's Vulkan is `vulkan:generic` only** (`thread_warp_size=1`, 256 threads, 32 KiB LDS): per dispatch it is `bindPipeline` + `vkCmdPushDescriptorSetWithTemplate` + push consts + dispatch + an unconditional barrier (~150 ns here), CBs are one-per-stream with a **synchronous busy fence wait** on flush (the anti-pattern we already beat), KV is paged at 16 tokens with a **block arc by depth** (≤5 attention calls per step) and device-side slot mapping, quant is weights-only group-32 `q4f16_1` with dequant fused into the matmul, prefill/decode share one symbolic-shape graph distinguished by `use_decode_kernel_` per depth plus **compile-time low-batch buckets [2,4]**, and the multi-GPU stack is NCCL TP/PP with no Vulkan transport. **The one thing to steal is D2's algorithm: `moe_matmul.group_gemm`'s persistent 1 024-CTA loop over an `indptr`-flattened `(expert, tile)` space** (BLK 8/128/32), plus the low-batch bucket idea for the 4–6 stream goal. Top-3: **X1 fix the counter window first** (it may delete the whole "recording is 22 %" workstream), **X2 the three host-side fixes** (266 → ~70 ns, 0.49 → 0.13 ms/token), **X3 the only real 30 ms: the `bpr` cold-load chain, with `UNROLL`/software prefetch interleaved against the real expert GEMV.**
* **10 — the 4 700 is not reachable by fusion and the 6.7 µs/barrier is not safe to build on; the lever is legs, and the biggest leg consumer is the hyper-connection projector.** Static census of the decode path (every kernel entry is one `record()`) gives **32 dispatches per GDN block / 41 per QSA block = ~1.65 k/token** (+210 for 30 placed blocks), so 4 700/48 = 98 per block is impossible from this code — the counter adds a whole batch's count per flush and divides by generated tokens only, so a prompt executed one token at a time inflates it; Descriptors are free money on this driver — `VK_EXT_descriptor_buffer` (16 B/descriptor, 8 388 606 SSBOs/stage), `VK_KHR_push_descriptor` rev2 `bufferlessPushDescriptors`, `VK_EXT_descriptor_heap` are all advertised on all three devices; bindless + a push-constant base index makes per-dispatch descriptor work **zero Vulkan calls** vs today's `allocate + N writes + bind`. * **08 — the submit count is not the lever, and round 7's CB boundary was a measurement bug.** Everything priced on the headless RX, interleaved so clock drift cancels, with the two traps named: **the RX is pinned at 500 MHz** under `power_dpm_force_performance_level = auto` (polled during a run; round 6's 5.09 TFLOP/s is *exactly* the 500 MHz FP32 peak and 28 % of the 1801 MHz one — so "the GPU half is 70.8 ms" may be a clock artefact, now Top-3 #1), and r07's `chain` arm wrote **overlapping query slots** (`base` 0/3 with `per=4`), so its "CB boundary = 21–34 µs" was really *three dispatches plus a boundary*: re-measured with disjoint slots, **a CB boundary is 1.7 µs of GPU timeline, identical for secondary and primary CBs** (12 secondaries: 30.8 → 50.0 µs, +1.75 each), while a dispatch+barrier in the same window is 2.57 µs. Submission economics are flat and small: **fence-waited submit 33.2 µs (submit 8.6 + wait 23.8), queued submit 8.6 µs wall / +8.8 µs GPU**, so 13.6 submits/block-chunk (654 submits per chunk-64, §8af) is **21.6 ms per chunk = 0.34 ms/token**, not the ~34 ms/token §8ah compared it to (a per-chunk number set beside per-token rows). **The one place the submit path pays is re-upload**: replaying a frozen CB is 39 µs cheaper per 12-dispatch round than re-recording it, and the delta scales to **~1.4–1.8 µs/dispatch at 12–32 dispatches** — invisible to `record_seconds()` because it happens in `vkQueueSubmit`, and worth ~6.7 ms/token of prefill *if* it holds at engine scale (Top-3 #2). **The interleave is worth 10× the submit-count item**: with an 800 µs host slice per block, `submit → host work → wait` measures **905 → 811 µs/block** (≈4.5 ms/token at 48 blocks) against today's submit-then-wait. Secondary CBs are legal and cheap with one hard rule — a barrier *inside* a secondary **does** order across secondaries on RADV (**12/12** dependent increments, sync-validation silent) and so does one in the primary, but with **no** barrier the answer is wrong (**11/12**, **0/12**), so take the spec-safe primary-side barrier (same cost). `VK_EXT_device_generated_commands` (Q3): **every creation step works** (`IndirectExecutionSet`, 2-token layout, 6 656 B preprocess requirement) and **execution faults** — `[gfxhub] page fault` at a null-ish VA then `ring gfx_0.0.0 timeout`, recovered by reset, twice (before and after adding `bufferDeviceAddress`, which was a real omission) — and structurally it can never help, being a *recorded command*. **DeepSpeed-Inference** (clone OK, `1fcac82`) is a TP *training* family with an inference facade: 5 hand-written kernels/layer (`LN+QKV`, attention, `o_proj+residual`, `gate+up+GeLU` in one GEMM, `down+residual(+allreduce)`), KV partitioned **by head, no paging** in v1 and block-allocated in v2; what transfers is **ZeRO-Inference's NVMe streaming** (pinned ring, 1 MiB blocks, queue depth 8 — independently matching our §03 queue limit), v2's **reserve-then-admit scheduler with typed refusals**, and the **host-built tile map** (`AtomBuilder`) pattern; their 70B-on-NVMe design point is 6–7 tok/s aggregate at batch 96 (throughput-mode, ~14 s/step) and their MoE design assumes fully-resident experts, so **nothing there touches our residency/placement problem**. * **06 — GEMM shapes priced + ncnn, and crash safety re-grounded.** The **shipping geometry is already optimal**: `local_size_x 256` / 8 rows per workgroup / subgroup 64 is best-or-tied in every stable cell, pricing the shared expert's gate/up at **5.09 TFLOP/s and 179 GB/s** (0.92 MB of weights, Infinity-Cache resident, so not bandwidth bound) and `down` at **2.83 TFLOP/s**; * **03 — IO/page-cache + exllamav2.** The model-bigger-than-RAM problem is **one sysctl, mis-set**: `read_ahead_kb` must be written to the **btrfs BDI** (`/sys/class/bdi/btrfs-1/`), not the block device's queue (writing the latter changes nothing), and 8192 → **128 KiB** takes cold expert reads from **0.7–1.1 to 4.7–5.4 GB/s** with amplification **9× → 1.05×** — i.e. a cold run 28 → 4 s and the merged-prefill byte floor from 21 to ~140 tok/s/agent. * **04 — launch count, priced + LMDeploy/TurboMind.** The launch-count gap is **not a launch problem**: a dispatch with nothing to wait on costs **0.007 µs (RX) / 0.031 µs (MI50)** of GPU timeline, but one compute→compute barrier after each costs **0.650 / 2.867 µs**, and `dispatch_batch::record` emits exactly one barrier per dispatch — so our 1 509 dispatches/token buy **0.98 ms (RX) / 4.32 ms (MI50)** of serialisation = 1.4–6.1 % of the 70.8 ms GPU half, and **round 3's headline `vkCmdDispatchIndirect` is refuted**: indirect costs **+0.77 µs (RX) / +1.65 µs (MI50)** per dispatch (1.418 vs 0.650; 4.510 vs 2.867), i.e. −1.2…−2.5 ms/token if adopted. Secondary-CB replay works on RADV (descriptor-set swap only, 19.6 ns/set) but is worth **−0.3 ms/token** (recording is really 68 ns/dispatch, not 212 — the difference is a `vkAllocateDescriptorSets` per dispatch); `VK_EXT_device_generated_commands` is **fully present on both cards with COMPUTE pipeline binding** (mesa 26.2.4) and still worthless here because every token it emits is an indirect dispatch. What does pay: **barrier pruning** (1 509 → ~400, bit-identical, one flag, −0.7…−3.2 ms/token) and **fused MoE** (31 → 8 launches/block, `n_ff = 640` means one workgroup per (expert,row) with 12.6 KiB LDS, ≤4 rows in 64 KiB) — and, being a placement rule, the MI50's boundary is 4.4× the RX's, so dependent fine-grained work belongs on the RX. LMDeploy contributes **prefetch-before-wait scheduling** (build token t+1 while t is on the GPU), **`prefill_interval = 16`** fairness, **KV over-subscription with swap maps/eviction**, **prefix + decode-state checkpointing**, and the structural confirmation that their CUDA graph exists to freeze *geometry* (token buckets), not to remove barriers; their `deep_gemm`/sm90/TMA/DeepEP stack has no Vulkan equivalent and no need. Prefill's real cost is per-group (429–530 µs streamed vs 67–84 resident), and launch count is 1–4 % of it.
* **05 — PLE gather/IO priced + TensorRT-LLM.** For 90-byte records, **`vkCmdCopyBuffer2` region arrays are the worst option measured**: **~300–360 ns/region (MI50) / 110–140 ns (RX)**, nearly independent of region size (6 144 rows = 2 316 µs / 857 µs), because the copy engine serialises one packet per region — the "batch the small copies" instinct is 40–350× *slower* than a compute shader. A **compute-shader gather costs load-instruction count, not bytes**: 45 × `uint16` (byte-exact 90 B) = **7.4 ns/row (MI50) / 2.3 ns/row (RX)**; the same data **padded to 96 B and read as 6 × `uvec4`** = **1.0 / 0.34 ns/row (77 / 234 GB/s payload)** — **7.4×/6.8× for +6.7 % storage**, and wider ILP does not help. Where the source lives dominates: VRAM 1–3 ns/row, but **system RAM over PCIe 46.9 / 64.6 ns/row** — *and `HOST_VISIBLE|DEVICE_LOCAL` "ReBAR" is VRAM, not host memory*, which invalidates the obvious version of that experiment; a warm 32-thread host gather (~16 ns/row amortised) beats GPU-over-PCIe, so the GPU may only gather what is already local. On the IO side the **48× over-read is one 4 KiB block, not a compressed extent**: FIEMAP shows **2 213 extents of 8–16 MiB, physical == logical (uncompressed)**, a random 90-B `pread` costs 63 µs and pulls 4.07 KiB, and FIEMAP coalescing is a dead end (6 144 random rows land in ~2 213 extents ⇒ merge 3 of 1 024 regions; a 13 MB extent read to use 2.8 rows). The fix is layout: **token-major 4 320-B (48 × 90 B) records measure 8.18 KiB/read = 1.9×** and take a 128-token chunk from **15.6 ms → 0.45 ms at 32 threads (34×, 24× fewer bytes)** — matching the engine's measured 14.7 ms chunked-PLE regression. 8 threads already reach 62 % of 32-thread IO throughput, so the PLE gather wants 8–16 threads. Map churn is 2.8–3.4 µs per map+unmap vs 6–8 ns/write persistent; `VK_EXT_host_image_copy` is absent on the MI50 and `VK_KHR_map_memory2` changes nothing. **TensorRT-LLM** (PyTorch-first now; policy still in `batch_manager/`) contributes three structural ideas: a **per-iteration token budget charged in *compute* tokens net of the radix-tree-reusable prefix** (`microBatchScheduler.cpp:405,67`) with chunks quantised to the KV block (`chunkUnitSize`) and **`kEQUAL_PROGRESS` lock-step fairness**; **tiered KV (GPU/HOST/DISK, quota per tier, priority-LRU eviction per level, transcode-on-demote)** whose *shape* fits our expert residency better than it fits KV; and **token-count-bucketed piecewise CUDA graphs** whose only portable residue is the bucketing discipline (mechanism is CUDA-only; our replay ceiling is −0.3 ms/token from round 4). Plus `SpeculationGate` (rolling accept-rate → permanent disable, ~20 lines) to ship with any future draft path.
* **06 — small-M GEMM shapes priced + ncnn.** Launch *geometry* is not a lever: 64→512 threads (2→16 rows/WG) is flat within **5 %** on both cards for the shared expert's two shapes, so the current `ls256/r8` stays and the chunked pass must not be re-tuned around launch shape; the only cheap win is **`UNROLL=2` on the block loop (−9 % gate/up m=1, −8 % m=8, −9 % down m=1, neutral-to-worse at `bpr=3`)**. **LDS staging of the activation rows is a loss everywhere (+6 %…2.6×)**. Two measurement traps set the prices honestly: an un-warmed first dispatch reads **5× slow** (2.39 µs vs 12.4 µs for the same 640×2560 m=1 GEMM), and 64 back-to-back identical dispatches **overlap** (137 ns) where a *dependent* chain pays **1.07 µs per barrier** (v2 `COMPUTE→COMPUTE` is 1.6× cheaper than our v1 `ALL_COMMANDS`; `write→read` == `read|write`; `BY_REGION` −4 %), so the parked pruning item is worth **−1.0 ms/token** at 1509 → ~400 barriers — and ncnn's **per-resource tracked `stage`/`access` flags** (`command.cpp:2214`) is the pattern that removes the call-site ≠ dispatch blocker. **gate+up+SwiGLU fused into one dispatch is the round's biggest win: 15.6 → 4.7–5.0 µs (m=1) and 50.7 → 9.3 µs (m=8), 3.1×/5.3× on the dependent path**, because a dependent GEMM costs 5.35 µs as a chain leg but 2.79 µs back-to-back; **SwiGLU+`down` in one dispatch is infeasible** (needs all 1.84 MB through one CU; Vulkan has no grid sync), so "chunk-level" means 2 dispatches, not 1. At m=8 the shared expert's three GEMMs are **18.8 µs/block = 0.90 ms/token over 48 blocks** against ~5.8 ms/token in the T=1 trace — the rest is dependency structure, not kernel time. **`VK_KHR_cooperative_matrix` is absent on both cards** (settles round 1), the RX's default subgroup **is 64** (forcing 32 costs 5–8 %), the MI50 is **3.0–5.2× slower** on exactly these shapes, and the untried features that matter are **shape-specialised pipelines** (ncnn freezes shape + `local_size` per pipeline and persists the cache — our unroll-2 win is its floor), **`VK_KHR_pipeline_binary`** (kills the cold-start compile behind both the 5× trap and the 24-fresh-shapes reset), and **`VK_EXT_descriptor_buffer`**. ncnn's mobile baggage — storage images, `elempack`, winograd, and the Adreno-bug workarounds sprinkled through `convolution_vulkan.cpp` — does not transfer. **Reprice one number (`06a`, second independent harness, IQ4_XS):** the dependent-chain barrier cost is **flat from 1 to 320 workgroups** and tracks **`bpr`** instead (1.15 µs + ≈0.45 µs per dependent block ⇒ `bpr` 2/3/10 = 1.6/2.5/5.6 µs), so it is **cache invalidation, not wave-drain**, and a pruned `bpr=10` barrier is worth **≈5.6 µs, i.e. ≈−4.5 ms/token at 1509 → ~400** (not −1.0); barrier *form* (sync2 vs `ALL_COMMANDS`) measured identical on both harnesses' working sets, so narrow the masks for hygiene, not for milliseconds.

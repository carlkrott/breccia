# Paired research (standing rotation)

Every implementation / test round is paired with **one research subagent** that does two things:

1. **Vulkan capability audit** — anything in the driver/extension/parameter surface we are not exploiting, tied to a current bottleneck.
2. **One engine audit** — a single inference engine (not yet covered) reviewed for workflows, methods and specific advantages we have not tried, with an adaptation sketch for our Vulkan engine.

Findings land in `docs/research/NN-<engine>.md`. This index tracks coverage so we never re-audit the same engine twice.

Round verdicts (one line each, so a future round knows what was learned without re-reading):

* **07 — attribution tooling priced, and the §8ac mystery is a boundary cost.** On this box there is **no hardware-counter and no profiler path**: `VK_KHR_performance_query` is advertised with **17 counters on qf0 only** (0 on the dedicated compute family) but `vkCmdBeginQuery` on a performance pool **segfaults in `libvulkan_radeon.so`** (mesa 26.2.4, reproduced from raw C and from ash; box healthy), and **RGP is impossible** (0 `rgp` symbols in `/proc/kallsyms`, no `/dev/rgp`); `umr`/`radeontop`/`amdtop`/`perf` are not installed (`perf_event_paranoid=2`, passwordless sudo available). What works is enough: **a timestamp costs 306 ns** (256 stamps = 77.96 µs, flat; v1 `TOP/BOTTOM_OF_PIPE` costs +210 ns more than v2 `COMPUTE_SHADER`), so **20 stages × 2 stamps = 12 µs = 0.06 %** of a 20 ms token while per-*dispatch* stamping is +5.1/6.5/7.3 % at 4/16/64 dispatches (+560–650 ns each) and needs its own build. **The unit trap**: RADV's calibrated timestamps are **nanoseconds**, query timestamps are **ticks** (`×10` on the RX, `×40` on the MI50 — verified against `/proc/uptime`), and with them anchored the device clock holds to a **521 ns sample spread / 52–143 ns declared deviation**. Attribution then splits cleanly into four measured costs: **a submit+fence has a fixed 56 µs** (empty CB: record 0.1 / submit 7.2 / **wait 56.1** / total 63.4 µs against a 0.4 µs GPU span, and `wait ≈ GPU span + 56 µs` at every size, so batching 12 dispatches costs the same fixed 71 µs as batching 1), it is **not host wake-up** (spin-polling `vkGetFenceStatus` gives 54.5 vs 57.6 µs), **a command-buffer boundary costs 21–34 µs of GPU timeline even inside one submit** (21 µs survives removing every barrier, so it is prologue/epilogue, +12 µs is the trailing `ALL_COMMANDS` drain — 5–8× a dispatch), and **each extra submit adds +62–65 µs wall with +9 µs GPU**; a barrier's own price tracks the **dirty footprint** (3.56 µs at 256 KiB, knee at ~1 MiB, 5.68 µs at 16 MiB; `ALL_COMMANDS` == v2 within 0.1 %). That closes §8ac arithmetically: removing 167 of 192 shared-expert dispatches recovered 2.7 ms = **16 µs per dispatch**, which is exactly the measured per-dispatch cost — §8ab's "two orders" came from pricing *kernel time* and assuming launch cost was per-dispatch, when it is per-**boundary** (~300 synchronous boundaries or a handful of 100× stages are the only two shapes that fit 18 ms, distinguished in one run by the `host / GPU / host−GPU` table). `VK_AMD_buffer_marker` works as a progress probe (+682 ns/marker, u32 only), `RADV_DEBUG=shaderstats|info|hang` and `ACO_DEBUG=validateir|validatera` are the remaining driver levers. **Ollama** (clone OK, 6c15b26) is now a Go supervisor over a **`llama-server` subprocess per model**, so all batching/KV/FA is llama.cpp-inherited; what it adds is a **runner scheduler** (ref-counted, reload-on-option-change via `needsReload`, keep-alive `expireTimer`/`sessionDuration` resettable per request, `maxRunners = 3 × GPUs` as a *latency* guard, bounded `pendingReqCh` backpressure), a **predict-then-degrade load loop** (`updateFreeSpace` + `reduceAutoNumCtxForLoadOOM`, degrading only auto knobs and remembering which were auto), and **per-GPU VRAM accounting scraped from the child's `buffer size` log lines** (with a startup warning when the format drifts) — ranked adaptations: residency lease with idle expiry, predict-then-degrade admission with a bounded queue, engine-local alloc accounting reconciled against `mem_info_vram_used`.

* **06 — GEMM shapes priced + ncnn, and crash safety re-grounded.** The **shipping geometry is already optimal**: `local_size_x 256` / 8 rows per workgroup / subgroup 64 is best-or-tied in every stable cell, pricing the shared expert's gate/up at **5.09 TFLOP/s and 179 GB/s** (0.92 MB of weights, Infinity-Cache resident, so not bandwidth bound) and `down` at **2.83 TFLOP/s**; forcing subgroup 32 and staging activations in LDS both *lose*, and at m=8 the launch shape is irrelevant to within 0.5 % (all three geometries land at 22.5 µs gate/up) — the prefill path has nothing to gain from geometry and everything to gain from fewer dispatches. **The method was the hidden bug**: a "time N dispatches in a loop" protocol moved the same cell from 2 616 to 12 875 ns between runs and inverted the geometry ranking, so only the **dependent-chain** protocol (barrier per dispatch, 150×3, spread ≤1.02) is admissible — and in that protocol **one dependent barrier costs 3.43 µs (sync2) / 6.25 µs (v1)** at a 256 KiB working set, i.e. 5–10× round 4's 0.65 µs because the cost tracks the dirty L2 footprint; access-mask narrowing and `BY_REGION` are 0.3 %, so ~1 100 dependent dispatches/token are worth **~3.8 ms** and barrier pruning deserves un-parking. **Fused gate+up+SwiGLU measures 2.8×** (64.2 → 22.7 µs at m=1) with no LDS and no spill risk, but **`down` cannot join it**: the LDS budget fits (20 KiB at m=8) and is not the constraint — `down`'s 2 560 rows all need the 640 intermediates, so one workgroup would read both whole matrices at parallelism 1. **Crash safety outranks all of it**: the 23:44 reset was a **ring timeout with one outstanding packet** (not 447 faults — the 138 `no-retry page faults` 17 min earlier belong to `vkgather`, an OOB, and the box survived them), and `gemm_many` was blocked in `vkWaitForFences(u64::MAX)`, so the only decider left was the kernel's watchdog, whose recovery on the display card is a **BACO reset**. **The pipeline-storm hypothesis is refuted**: 128 fresh pipelines cold-cache = 117 ms of pure host ACO compilation, zero GPU work, zero failures, 42× faster warm (0.03 ms/pipeline) — creation cannot produce a `ring gfx timeout`; what a storm costs is wall-clock. What does convert a hang into a `VkResult`-shaped event is **abandoning the process** (`RADV_DEBUG=hang`'s 81 ms deadline aborted a run and left the device healthy, busy 0, no kernel messages), whereas an unbounded fence wait hands the decision to the kernel; `VK_EXT_robustness2` is present and costs **≤1 %** on the real GEMM so OOB becomes clamp/zero instead of a page fault, malformed SPIR-V **aborts the process even with the validation layer complaining** (only build-time `spirv-val` protects), and `/sys/class/drm/card*/device/devcoredump/` appears *before* the reset and is the pre-detectable signal. **ncnn** (clone OK, 9fc8106) contributes three structural items, none kernel-level: **one command buffer + one submit per forward pass** (`net.cpp:305`), **push descriptors via `vkCmdPushDescriptorSetWithTemplateKHR`** (`command.cpp:1432`, no descriptor allocation per dispatch), and **shape specialisation constants + a persisted `VkPipelineCache` with header validation** (`convolution_vulkan.cpp:499`, `pipelinecache.cpp:347`) instead of our one-SPIR-V-per-geometry, which is what makes a many-shape sweep cheap; it has *no* barrier-elision pass (same per-op barrier as us) and its mobile assumptions (elempack 4, fp16 default, vendor subgroup table, tile-GPU workgroup heuristics) do not transfer.
* **01 — MLC-LLM.** Its Vulkan backend is a SteamDeck-*portability* target (`thread_warp_size=1`, no subgroup ops, no pipeline cache), so nothing in its kernels is worth porting; the transferable value is compile-time memory planning, an `indptr`-driven single-dispatch group MoE, and radix-tree prefix reuse with COW sequence forking. On our side the audit found f32 **storage** atomics do exist on both cards (`atomicExchange`; `atomicAdd` does not, but **shared-memory f32 `atomicAdd` does**), a free `RADV_EXPERIMENTAL=transfer_queue` SDMA family for the streaming lane, and that **21–29 ms of the 70.8 ms GPU half is not arithmetic** (per-dispatch `ALL_COMMANDS` barrier).
* **02 — measured follow-ups + PowerInfer.** Barriers are **free as packets** (0.01 µs); what costs 1.1–4.4 µs/dispatch is the *launch serialization* they force, so `full→sync2` is worth **−2.5…−6.2 ms/token** and nothing more — the 21–29 ms attribution of round 1 is **refuted**, and event-based waits, wave32 (0.1 % on a latency probe), the transfer queue as a global flag (8.6× *slower* for on-card copies on the MI50) and denormal modes all measured ≈0 or negative. What does pay: `vkCmdCopyBuffer2` region arrays (**0.13 µs vs 1.0–1.5 µs of host record per region**, −5…−8 ms/chunk of prefill host time), ~13–15 µs per queue submission, and PowerInfer's two structural ideas — **per-neuron ILP residency** (`gpu_idx`+`gpu_bucket`, 75 % of FFN in 12.4 GiB) and **CPU node ‖ GPU node joined by one `add`**, which is the shape that removes our 38–50 ms host lane from the critical path.
* **03 — IO/page-cache + exllamav2.** The model-bigger-than-RAM problem is **one sysctl, mis-set**: `read_ahead_kb` must be written to the **btrfs BDI** (`/sys/class/bdi/btrfs-1/`), not the block device's queue (writing the latter changes nothing), and 8192 → **128 KiB** takes cold expert reads from **0.7–1.1 to 4.7–5.4 GB/s** with amplification **9× → 1.05×** — i.e. a cold run 28 → 4 s and the merged-prefill byte floor from 21 to ~140 tok/s/agent. With that right, `O_DIRECT` (5.9 GB/s cold but 10× worse than the 58 GB/s warm page-cache path), `FADV_RANDOM`/`MADV_RANDOM` (llama.cpp's env knobs: neutral-to-*negative* on btrfs and on 462–900 KiB regions), `MADV_SEQUENTIAL`, prefetch depth > 2 (queue-limited: `nr_requests 256 × max_sectors 128 KiB = 32 MiB` ≈ 1.7 blocks) and `MADV_POPULATE_READ` (3.9 GB/s, blocking) are all dominated. The PLE shard is a *different* pathology: one 28.8 GiB tensor of **90-byte records**, 65 µs per cold row, 48× btrfs-extent over-read, 0.5 µs warm — residency + 32-way parallel gather, not advice. exllamav2 contributes exactly two things: the **measure-per-module-then-anneal-the-bit-budget** allocation method (only usable for KV — our weights are a fixed artifact behind a bit-identical gate) and the structural fact that its per-layer launch count is **~2 against our ~31**, whose Vulkan form is **`vkCmdDispatchIndirect` over a pre-recorded command buffer** (a replayable "graph" without the 6-deep ring) on top of the fused MoE already queued as item 7. Its CUDA graphs, fp8 KV and `bits_prop` mixed-rate format have no Vulkan equivalent and matter little.
* **04 — launch count, priced + LMDeploy/TurboMind.** The launch-count gap is **not a launch problem**: a dispatch with nothing to wait on costs **0.007 µs (RX) / 0.031 µs (MI50)** of GPU timeline, but one compute→compute barrier after each costs **0.650 / 2.867 µs**, and `dispatch_batch::record` emits exactly one barrier per dispatch — so our 1 509 dispatches/token buy **0.98 ms (RX) / 4.32 ms (MI50)** of serialisation = 1.4–6.1 % of the 70.8 ms GPU half, and **round 3's headline `vkCmdDispatchIndirect` is refuted**: indirect costs **+0.77 µs (RX) / +1.65 µs (MI50)** per dispatch (1.418 vs 0.650; 4.510 vs 2.867), i.e. −1.2…−2.5 ms/token if adopted. Secondary-CB replay works on RADV (descriptor-set swap only, 19.6 ns/set) but is worth **−0.3 ms/token** (recording is really 68 ns/dispatch, not 212 — the difference is a `vkAllocateDescriptorSets` per dispatch); `VK_EXT_device_generated_commands` is **fully present on both cards with COMPUTE pipeline binding** (mesa 26.2.4) and still worthless here because every token it emits is an indirect dispatch. What does pay: **barrier pruning** (1 509 → ~400, bit-identical, one flag, −0.7…−3.2 ms/token) and **fused MoE** (31 → 8 launches/block, `n_ff = 640` means one workgroup per (expert,row) with 12.6 KiB LDS, ≤4 rows in 64 KiB) — and, being a placement rule, the MI50's boundary is 4.4× the RX's, so dependent fine-grained work belongs on the RX. LMDeploy contributes **prefetch-before-wait scheduling** (build token t+1 while t is on the GPU), **`prefill_interval = 16`** fairness, **KV over-subscription with swap maps/eviction**, **prefix + decode-state checkpointing**, and the structural confirmation that their CUDA graph exists to freeze *geometry* (token buckets), not to remove barriers; their `deep_gemm`/sm90/TMA/DeepEP stack has no Vulkan equivalent and no need. Prefill's real cost is per-group (429–530 µs streamed vs 67–84 resident), and launch count is 1–4 % of it.
* **05 — PLE gather/IO priced + TensorRT-LLM.** For 90-byte records, **`vkCmdCopyBuffer2` region arrays are the worst option measured**: **~300–360 ns/region (MI50) / 110–140 ns (RX)**, nearly independent of region size (6 144 rows = 2 316 µs / 857 µs), because the copy engine serialises one packet per region — the "batch the small copies" instinct is 40–350× *slower* than a compute shader. A **compute-shader gather costs load-instruction count, not bytes**: 45 × `uint16` (byte-exact 90 B) = **7.4 ns/row (MI50) / 2.3 ns/row (RX)**; the same data **padded to 96 B and read as 6 × `uvec4`** = **1.0 / 0.34 ns/row (77 / 234 GB/s payload)** — **7.4×/6.8× for +6.7 % storage**, and wider ILP does not help. Where the source lives dominates: VRAM 1–3 ns/row, but **system RAM over PCIe 46.9 / 64.6 ns/row** — *and `HOST_VISIBLE|DEVICE_LOCAL` "ReBAR" is VRAM, not host memory*, which invalidates the obvious version of that experiment; a warm 32-thread host gather (~16 ns/row amortised) beats GPU-over-PCIe, so the GPU may only gather what is already local. On the IO side the **48× over-read is one 4 KiB block, not a compressed extent**: FIEMAP shows **2 213 extents of 8–16 MiB, physical == logical (uncompressed)**, a random 90-B `pread` costs 63 µs and pulls 4.07 KiB, and FIEMAP coalescing is a dead end (6 144 random rows land in ~2 213 extents ⇒ merge 3 of 1 024 regions; a 13 MB extent read to use 2.8 rows). The fix is layout: **token-major 4 320-B (48 × 90 B) records measure 8.18 KiB/read = 1.9×** and take a 128-token chunk from **15.6 ms → 0.45 ms at 32 threads (34×, 24× fewer bytes)** — matching the engine's measured 14.7 ms chunked-PLE regression. 8 threads already reach 62 % of 32-thread IO throughput, so the PLE gather wants 8–16 threads. Map churn is 2.8–3.4 µs per map+unmap vs 6–8 ns/write persistent; `VK_EXT_host_image_copy` is absent on the MI50 and `VK_KHR_map_memory2` changes nothing. **TensorRT-LLM** (PyTorch-first now; policy still in `batch_manager/`) contributes three structural ideas: a **per-iteration token budget charged in *compute* tokens net of the radix-tree-reusable prefix** (`microBatchScheduler.cpp:405,67`) with chunks quantised to the KV block (`chunkUnitSize`) and **`kEQUAL_PROGRESS` lock-step fairness**; **tiered KV (GPU/HOST/DISK, quota per tier, priority-LRU eviction per level, transcode-on-demote)** whose *shape* fits our expert residency better than it fits KV; and **token-count-bucketed piecewise CUDA graphs** whose only portable residue is the bucketing discipline (mechanism is CUDA-only; our replay ceiling is −0.3 ms/token from round 4). Plus `SpeculationGate` (rolling accept-rate → permanent disable, ~20 lines) to ship with any future draft path.
* **06 — small-M GEMM shapes priced + ncnn.** Launch *geometry* is not a lever: 64→512 threads (2→16 rows/WG) is flat within **5 %** on both cards for the shared expert's two shapes, so the current `ls256/r8` stays and the chunked pass must not be re-tuned around launch shape; the only cheap win is **`UNROLL=2` on the block loop (−9 % gate/up m=1, −8 % m=8, −9 % down m=1, neutral-to-worse at `bpr=3`)**. **LDS staging of the activation rows is a loss everywhere (+6 %…2.6×)**. Two measurement traps set the prices honestly: an un-warmed first dispatch reads **5× slow** (2.39 µs vs 12.4 µs for the same 640×2560 m=1 GEMM), and 64 back-to-back identical dispatches **overlap** (137 ns) where a *dependent* chain pays **1.07 µs per barrier** (v2 `COMPUTE→COMPUTE` is 1.6× cheaper than our v1 `ALL_COMMANDS`; `write→read` == `read|write`; `BY_REGION` −4 %), so the parked pruning item is worth **−1.0 ms/token** at 1509 → ~400 barriers — and ncnn's **per-resource tracked `stage`/`access` flags** (`command.cpp:2214`) is the pattern that removes the call-site ≠ dispatch blocker. **gate+up+SwiGLU fused into one dispatch is the round's biggest win: 15.6 → 4.7–5.0 µs (m=1) and 50.7 → 9.3 µs (m=8), 3.1×/5.3× on the dependent path**, because a dependent GEMM costs 5.35 µs as a chain leg but 2.79 µs back-to-back; **SwiGLU+`down` in one dispatch is infeasible** (needs all 1.84 MB through one CU; Vulkan has no grid sync), so "chunk-level" means 2 dispatches, not 1. At m=8 the shared expert's three GEMMs are **18.8 µs/block = 0.90 ms/token over 48 blocks** against ~5.8 ms/token in the T=1 trace — the rest is dependency structure, not kernel time. **`VK_KHR_cooperative_matrix` is absent on both cards** (settles round 1), the RX's default subgroup **is 64** (forcing 32 costs 5–8 %), the MI50 is **3.0–5.2× slower** on exactly these shapes, and the untried features that matter are **shape-specialised pipelines** (ncnn freezes shape + `local_size` per pipeline and persists the cache — our unroll-2 win is its floor), **`VK_KHR_pipeline_binary`** (kills the cold-start compile behind both the 5× trap and the 24-fresh-shapes reset), and **`VK_EXT_descriptor_buffer`**. ncnn's mobile baggage — storage images, `elempack`, winograd, and the Adreno-bug workarounds sprinkled through `convolution_vulkan.cpp` — does not transfer. **Reprice one number (`06a`, second independent harness, IQ4_XS):** the dependent-chain barrier cost is **flat from 1 to 320 workgroups** and tracks **`bpr`** instead (1.15 µs + ≈0.45 µs per dependent block ⇒ `bpr` 2/3/10 = 1.6/2.5/5.6 µs), so it is **cache invalidation, not wave-drain**, and a pruned `bpr=10` barrier is worth **≈5.6 µs, i.e. ≈−4.5 ms/token at 1509 → ~400** (not −1.0); barrier *form* (sync2 vs `ALL_COMMANDS`) measured identical on both harnesses' working sets, so narrow the masks for hygiene, not for milliseconds.
